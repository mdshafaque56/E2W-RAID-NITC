What to fix first for electric two-wheelers

A decision framework that combines Thurstone scaling, TOPSIS and trust–risk modelling, applied to 201 online survey responses from NIT Calicut. Change the weights, switch blocks and pick a segment to test how firm each finding is.

Four steps from rankings to action

Measure

Thurstone Case V scales 31 statements in 9 forced-ranking blocks, with bootstrap intervals.

 2

Prioritise

TOPSIS ranks 12 trust levers and 19 risk barriers on four criteria.

Explain and act

Weight tests, resampling and a segment action matrix show what is firm.

      Does the ranking survive random weights?

      Draws 2,000 weight vectors uniformly from the simplex (Dirichlet with all parameters 1) and re-ranks each time. Darker cells mean that rank was reached more often. The last column is the chance of finishing in the top three.

      Read with care

      • The survey has no adoption-intention question, so adoption is proxied by trust, risk and the choice among three scooters.
      • The analysis uses 201 online responses; the offline half is not included yet.
      • The Conflicted segment has 8 people and is indicative only.
      • TOPSIS closeness is relative to the best item. Only the top two or three ranks are firm.
      • Scale values come from Thurstone Case V, not a full Thurstonian IRT model. Criteria and cut-offs are analyst choices.
